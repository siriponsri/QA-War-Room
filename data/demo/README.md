# Synthetic manufacturing CAPA demonstration fixtures

**These are original fictional engineering fixtures, not a published benchmark, real inspection records, independently reviewed pharmaceutical labels, or a source of GMP advice.** No external commercial records, real patient data, company documents or copyrighted regulatory text are embedded.

The task is to judge the exact case claim against its explicitly stated fictional internal protocol, within a declared scope and cutoff date. The three labels are `supported`, `contradicted` and `insufficient`. These labels do not mean certified compliant/noncompliant. Missing evidence is not automatically a manufacturing failure.

## Files and runtime separation

- `scenarios.json`: case descriptions, assessment claims, catalog metadata and budgets. The API exposes an explicit allowlist of public fields; template identifiers and research metadata are excluded.
- `evidence.json`: fictional source texts and structured source observations, held by the engine until their corresponding request is accepted. `available: false` represents an unsuccessful record request, not a negative laboratory result.
- `evaluator/gold.json`: authored reference labels, admissible reference support sets and explanations. Only the evaluator reads this file. Feedback is exposed after a terminal decision, never in the active policy observation.
- `engineering-smoke-results.json`: reproducible output of the bundled deterministic policies. Generated by `python -m qa_war_room benchmark --output data/demo/engineering-smoke-results.json`.

Anyone who can read this repository can read the references. The separation is designed to prevent accidental evaluator leakage through the app/API; it is not cryptographic concealment from a local developer. A research evaluation must use appropriately isolated held-out data and a separate evaluator process or service.

MFG-001 versus MFG-002 changes the effectiveness artifact only; the catalog and other source artifacts are identical. Other variants alter the artifact needed to create their stated condition. IDs/titles differ for navigation. These are matched engineering examples, not a statistically powered counterfactual study.

Do not randomly split these eight variants into training and test sets. Variants share nearly all language and structure. For a later benchmark, split entire originating cases, template families and source organizations as specified in the research protocol; then generate variants within the assigned group. These fixtures should remain development/smoke cases forever.

## Source records

Each case has eight catalog entries. A requested record costs its declared number of credits. A failed request also consumes its declared cost, because the record-search interaction occurred. Repeating the same request is idempotent and incurs no further cost. Every session has eight credits; the total catalog costs nine, so the utilities distractor can displace useful evidence.

Catalog titles, revisions and dates are assertions from the packet index, not proof that the source can be produced. In the missing-report case, the index lists a report but the custodian cannot provide it. Record availability is disclosed only when requested.

`facts` in a delivered record are structured source observations also described in its human-readable `content`. They allow transparent scripted policies to operate without an LLM. They are not evaluator labels. Neither field should be treated as an automatically reliable representation of real uploaded documents; future document extraction must be separately validated.

## Policy labels

The two `user-*` roles are scripted record custodians. The four `auditor-*` labels select deterministic acquisition orders or shortcuts. “Newbie” and “expert” preserve the original product-role idea; they do not establish experience, competence or equivalence to a human auditor.

- `auditor-newbie-01`: reads the cover sheet and trusts its claim. This intentionally weak baseline makes the danger of unsupported closure visible.
- `auditor-newbie-02`: reads in catalog order and applies the visible-data rules.
- `auditor-expert-01`: requests the protocol and required evidence before deciding.
- `auditor-expert-02`: prioritizes current status and counterevidence after reading the protocol.

These policies share one authored rule reader. Perfect performance on some fixtures is expected by construction and is not a finding about AI, multi-agent systems, pharmaceutical compliance or auditor learning.

## What the feedback does and does not measure

`verdict_correct` compares the submitted label to the authored reference. `evidence_support_complete` checks whether cited retrieved IDs cover at least one reference support set and whether any required unavailable-record query was actually made. `decision_and_support_correct` is their conjunction. This is a fixture-level coverage check, not semantic entailment or rationale grading. Extra context citations are listed separately and are not automatically false citations. A reference label may match by guessing even when support is incomplete.

Reports contain the submitted decision, a record-request checklist, source IDs/revisions/dates, event replay and terminal fixture feedback. The present code does not generate validated clause-by-clause regulatory findings, corrective-action recommendations, electronic signatures or a compliant audit trail.

## Extension boundary

Before claiming pharmaceutical applicability: independently review the protocol/labels, add real and/or independently authored held-out cases, document reference uncertainty, predeclare acquisition budgets and scoring, and compare meaningful strong baselines. Published open benchmark adapters and provider-backed LLM policies are subsequent work. No RL training, digital-twin synchronization or human learning study is implemented here.
